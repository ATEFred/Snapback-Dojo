## Part 1 — how story mode works

Wren the Unbothered (a fictional girl, deliberately NOT an avatar of anyone) walks
into the **Gloomwood** with the dojo pet at her side, because every weird thing in
there demands a reaction — and she intends to give them none. Sensei narrates.
The book has 8 chapters; each ends with a ridiculous monster defeated, a page in
**The Little Book of Former Weirdos** (sticker wall), and one quiet bridge line that
carries the lesson home.

### The battle mechanic
- An encounter is **3 taunts**. Each approved line (Sensei `okish` or `best`)
  empties one chip of the villain's **meter** — every villain has their own meter,
  tied to what they feed on (Echo's jar, Toastbrig's stew pot, the Duchess's spring…).
- **There is no fail state.** A wrong answer is never a loss and never shame — the
  villain gets *funnier*, explains (in story) why that answer made it worse, and she
  retries the **same taunt**. The story cannot stall.
- **🪄 Sensei's whisper**: after 2 tries on the same taunt, a free lifeline appears.
  Pick mode: the right line glows. Type mode: the line is borrowed into the box.
  Borrowing is always free — no coins, no penalty.
- **⭐ 1–3 stars** per chapter = bonus coins only. No gates, no lockouts.
- Cleared chapters replay as **rematches** (all lines typed, half coins).

### Why wrong answers are wrong (the doctrine, in fiction)
Every villain runs on a **fuel**, and every villain has **one inedible thing**:

| Ch | Villain | Feeds on | Inedible | Meter |
|----|---------|----------|----------|-------|
| 1 | Echo the Mimic Imp | reactions, twisted words | flat exact repetition | jar of stolen sounds |
| 2 | Toastbrig the Bridge Troll | novelty — any *new* material for the argument | bored agreement, naming the loop | opinion stew pot |
| 3 | The Porcelain Duchess of HA | laughter on command | dismissal, the yawn | mainspring wind |
| 4 | Wisp the Gossip Gremlin | you repeating or investigating the rumour | the one-question, the boring rewrite | jars of borrowed voices |
| 5 | The Sketch (Mirror Lake) | arguing with it, agreeing with its cruelty | true-and-kind lines, compliments to its face | graphite layers |
| 6 | Twin & Sprite | an audience, sides to pick | treated as one blob, watched by nobody | their audience seats |
| 7 | The Court of Mirrors | your appetite for the compliment | deflecting flattery, owing nothing | how low they bow |
| 8 | Queen Malvina | significance itself — being the main event | irrelevance. one line, then walking away | her throne's height |

The real-world shape underneath, never stated heavier than story:
**attention is the currency; you choose whether to pay.**

### Difficulty ramp (per chapter, beat by beat)

| Ch | Pick | Typed | Notes |
|----|------|-------|-------|
| 1 | 6 | 0 | everything multiple choice; teaches the Parrot |
| 2 | 4 | 2 | first typed answers at the END of scene 2; lenient grading (anything that isn't obviously fuel = a chip) |
| 3 | 3 | 3 | typed from mid-scene 1 |
| 4 | 2 | 4 | typed first; one lifeline beat where Echo whispers an example |
| 5 | 1 | 5 | the clone improvises against whatever she types (paired replies) |
| 6 | 0 | 6 | two speakers at once — typing one line answers both |
| 7 | 0 | 6 | flattery traps: *any* reply that accepts a title, bargains, or flatters back = fed |
| 8 | 0 | 7 | finale in 3 phases; the last one wants `best` but Sensei lends a line after 3 tries — completion is guaranteed |

Wrong-answer leniency stays kind early: Chapter 2 typed answers accept anything
dry; by Chapter 6 the grading is the same strictness as Drill mode.
Lifelines appear after 2 misses from Chapter 1 and after 1 miss in Chapters 6–8.

### The voices
- Villains speak with the **bully TTS voice**, Sensei with the **Sensei voice**;
  tap anywhere to continue. No timers. Ever.
- Sensei is a coach, not a therapist: dry, proud, occasionally ruthless about wording.

### The one honest bridge (ending doctrine)
Each chapter can end with one short Sensei line connecting wood-life to real-life.
They stay oblique — no school, no names, no scenario echoes — except the book's
final line (Chapter 8): **comebacks end scenes; grown-ups end campaigns.**

## Part 2 — the cast

- **Wren** — Burton-proportions child in a too-big ink-blue coat, wide wary eyes,
  composed mouth. Curious, slightly unnerved, absolutely not looking away. Unbothered
  is her *policy*, not her feelings — the eyes tell the truth, the mouth keeps the deal.
- **The pet** — the dojo pet (whatever she named it), grumpy, unimpressed, correct.
  Trotting companion; rescued-with-a-bow in Chapter 7; wears the spare crown in the finale.
- **Sensei** — narration, coaching, and the book's dry moral arithmetic.
- **Echo the Mimic Imp** — Chapter 1 villain → Chapter 2+ companion-spy. Patchwork
  rag doll, mismatched button eyes, grin one stitch too wide, pockets with holes
  (emotional ones).
- **Queen Malvina the Magnificent** — the arc villain of chapters 4–8. Tall, gothic,
  never shouts. Collects "moments when someone shrank" (her throne is literally
  upholstered with folded invitations). Cannot survive being ignored.

### Chapter 1 · The Stolen Meow — ✅ SHIPPED
**Teaches:** The Parrot. **Villain:** Echo the Mimic Imp (eats reactions;
flat repeats are indigestible). **Meter:** jar of stolen sounds (♪ → ✨ escapes).

- **Intro:** almost-bedtime wood; Echo catches the cat's MROW out of the air;
  Sensei states the doctrine: *he eats reactions; a mimic cannot digest a mirror.*
- **Scene 1 — The Hollow of Borrowed Noise:** 3 pick-taunts (mocking the meow /
  "next I'm taking your hi" / "impress me"). Best = exact bored repeat, wait, let
  the silence work. Jar leaks; the meow escapes and lands slightly dented.
- **Scene 2 — The Imp Follows Back:** 3 pick-taunts about being a boring main
  character, forever-following, stealing her LAUGH. Same move; he hiccups up all
  14 stolen sounds.
- **Twist:** "Nobody ever talks TO me. Do you know how LOUD running is?" Echo
  joins as a terrifying spy who will lose most of the plots.
- **Bridge:** the monsters here are mean because they're bored and lonely —
  "It explains a lot. Here… and elsewhere."
- **Reward:** 🧵 *Echo, Reformed (Sort Of)* · +40 🪙 · 🌙 Gloomwood Novice trophy.

### Chapter 2 · The Opinion on the Bridge
**Teaches:** The Fog Wall + The Loop. **Villain:** **Toastbrig**, a mountain of a
troll stitched from bridge leftovers (door-plank back, teapot-kettle head), who
blocks the only bridge with exactly one opinion.
**Meter:** his **opinion stew pot** — one bubble per loop; a clean line pops one.

**Setup:** to cross into the deeper wood there is one bridge, and one troll, and the
troll has noticed her socks. Whatever happens, he never runs out of the same thing.

**Scene 1 — The Bridge.** (pick, pick, pick)
1. **Taunt (booming, delighted):** "STOP. Your socks are LOUD. I can hear them
   from the kettle. Do something about it."
   - *best:* "My socks are loud. They're wool. It happens." (agree flatly, keep walking)
   - *ok:* "Noted by the kettle. Moving on."
   - *wrong:* "They're NOT loud! They're totally normal socks!" (new material —
     pot bubbles; he savours the argument), or pulling at them to prove it.
   - *Feedback lesson:* he can't eat "yes" — a loop needs a wall to bounce off.
2. **Taunt (same opinion, new wrapping):** "LOUD socks. WRONG socks. socks of
   SUSPICIOUS LOUDNESS. I'm writing this down."
   - *best:* "That's the same thing you said before." (flat; name the loop; wait)
   - *ok:* "You've said that one already. Any new ones?"
   - *wrong:* "You can't WRITE that, that's not even—" (debate = feast) or
     "What else you got, then?" (inviting the loop).
3. **Taunt (loop visibly breaking — he repeats himself MID-sentence):**
   "Your socks are — your socks are — your socks are LOUD! …why do I feel like a
   kettle? ANSWER ME PROPERLY."
   - *best:* "You said that three times. It's the same size hat every time." (bored
     pity at the loop itself, not the insult)
   - *ok:* "Broken record. Lovely record. Broken."
   - *wrong:* defending the socks with ANY detail (he writes it down, delighted).
- **Scene win:** the pot pops its last bubble and deflates with a genuinely sad
  wheeze. Toastbrig sits down so hard the bridge apologises.

**Scene 2 — He Follows (to the bridge's far side, where the wood gets stranger).**
(pick, type, type — first typed lines; lenient)
4. **Taunt:** "FINE. Follow you, then. Tell everyone at the OTHER bridge that your
   socks needed defending. They'll LOVE that crowd."
   - *best (pick):* "Tell everyone. My socks are used to it now."
   - *ok:* "Two bridges, same socks. Wild day. Anyway—"
   - *wrong:* pleading, or explaining to him WHY they're normal.
5. **Taunt:** "I'LL EAT THE SOCKS. Then you'll HAVE to argue with me about it."
   - *type, lenient.* Accept: agreement/yawn ("Go ahead. They're itchy anyway."),
     loop-naming ("You keep saying new things that are the same thing.").
     Reject: horror/pleading/counter-insult about his face.
6. **Taunt (desperate, near tears):** "SAY SOMETHING *NEW*! Give me ONE new thing!
   I've been doing the socks since the KETTLE era!"
   - *type, lenient.* Accept: flat "No." / "Same socks. New day. Not your problem."
     Reject: helping him brainstorm insults, anything wounded.
- **Scene win:** he sits in the middle of the path, springless. Echo (from her
  shoulder) offers the insight: "He doesn't HATE the socks. He only owns one
  joke and it's the socks."

- **Twist:** Toastbrig asks to be let into the **Former Weirdos Support Circle**
  ("I'll bring the kettle. I can do a SECOND opinion. It'll be about soup.").
  He becomes the wood's rumor-tracker — big troll, tiny notebook.
- **Bridge (Sensei):** *"You can't fix someone's one-opinion stew. You can refuse
  to live in the pot. Bored 'yes' is the lid."*
- **Reward:** 🌉 *Toastbrig, Bridge-Adjacent* · +40 🪙 · trophy *Loop Breaker*.

### Chapter 3 · The Gallery of Giggles
**Teaches:** The Yawn + The Dismissal. **Villain:** **The Porcelain Duchess of HA** —
a wind-up aristocrat with a brass key in her back, who rules a portrait gallery of
painted smiles. She does not ask. She *commands*: laugh.
**Meter:** her **mainspring** — each dismissal unwinds one turn; the key turns
slower, slower…

**Setup:** the bridge road passes through a candlelit gallery where every portrait
is mid-laugh. At the end: the Duchess, wound tight, pointing a porcelain finger at
the door as if she's been waiting for an audience of one. Because she has.

**Scene 1 — The Command.** (pick, pick, type)
1. **Taunt (pointed, regal, zero warmth):** "You. Laugh."
   - *best:* "No." (the whole line; hold the look; the key clicks one turn back)
   - *ok:* "Hard pass. Terrible acoustics in here anyway."
   - *wrong:* a nervous "ha… ha?" (the KEY WHIRS AND REWINDS — she forces a
     do-over; feedback: a nervous laugh is still a payment), or "Why should I?!"
     (etiquette debate = five more commands).
2. **Taunt (louder, theatrical, portraits turning their heads):** "I said LAUGH.
   It's HILARIOUS. Everything in my presence is hilarious. BEGIN."
   - *best:* "Not funny. Devastating work ethic, though." (bored verdict, stay put)
   - *ok:* a long, slow, audible YAWN. (yes — the yawn IS the move)
   - *wrong:* "I don't HAVE to find things funny!" (arguing with a command is
     still obeying the topic).
3. **Taunt (leaning in, ice):** "Frown at me in my own gallery. In front of the
   family. You'll want to have laughed, little girl."
   - *type, lenient.* Accept: dismissal / flip — "Scary. Anyway, is there a
     shortcut through here?" / "Your gallery's very beige." Reject: fear, anger,
     negotiating, laughing.
- **Scene win:** the key slows to half speed mid-sentence; she completes the threat
  with an audible *tick… tick…* and has to rewind herself to finish it, offended
  by her own slowing.

**Scene 2 — She Rewinds.** (pick, type, type) — she discovers time-looping the room
to get her laugh: same taunts, nicer dresses, escalating.
4. **Taunt (sweet, new dress, same face):** "Welcome to the gallery! Round TWO.
   Everything I said is FUNNIER now. LAUGH."
   - *best:* "You said funnier. You didn't say funny." (verdict; the spring ticks down)
   - *ok:* "Same joke, new curtain."
   - *wrong:* engaging with the new dress ("…do you like it? she asks, hopeful —
     feedback: compliments to a command are still payment").
5. **Taunt (spinning the key herself, desperate):** "LAUGHER. LONGER. I'll wind
   until you DO. I have CENTURIES. I have a TEA SERVICE. LAUGH."
   - *type.* Accept: yawn/dismiss/"No" variants, walking past her mid-command.
     Reject: pleading ("please stop"), trying to reason ("people laugh when
     things ARE funny, it's not a—" — she rewinds to interrupt, delighted).
6. **Taunt (the final command, quiet and cold — the dangerous register):**
   "Laugh. Or I'll tell the whole wood you couldn't make anyone laugh. Alone.
   On purpose."
   - *type.* Accept: the flip — "That's not a threat, that's Tuesday." /
     dismissal — "Tell them. I'll be outside. Not laughing." Reject: hurt, anger,
     performance.
- **Scene win:** she stops mid-word. The key holds. For one long moment nothing
  moves — then she curtsies, very small, very polite: *"...thank you. It has been
  wound since 1774. I believe I'll sit."* She sits and stays sat. The portraits'
  painted smiles soften into normal smiles.
- **Twist:** the "family" portraits weren't laughing WITH her — they were
  commanded, forever. The wood's saddest gallery was a room full of people who
  never got to say no. (Comedic handling, gentle landing.)
- **Bridge (Sensei):** *"Her laugh was never yours to give. Nobody gets the knobs
  on your feelings without change due."*
- **Reward:** 🎀 *The Duchess (Wound Down)* · +40 🪙 · trophy *Still Waters* ·
  the gallery becomes a shortcut (Chapter 8 walk-past cameo: she waves, still sat).

### Chapter 4 · The Jar of Borrowed Voices
**Teaches:** The Gossip + The Reporter. **Villain:** **Wisp**, a jar-bound gossip
gremlin — shoulders up, tiny, voice like a paper bag — who delivers rumours as
fact. **Meter:** jars of borrowed voices — a clean answer lids one jar.
**Arc role:** first breadcrumb of **the Queen**.

**Setup:** deeper wood, trees hung with little notices. Echo gets twitchy:
"Someone's been buying words in this wood. Big money. Small change."

**Scene 1 — The Delivery.** (pick, type, type)
1. **Taunt (whisper that carries):** "Everyone says you talk to your cat. In CODE.
   I saw. Well — everyone saw. I'm everyone now."
   - *best (pick):* "Who exactly says that?" (one question, flat, judge-curious —
     then silence; a jar LIDS itself with a satisfying *pop*)
   - *ok:* "They said nothing. Check your sources."
   - *wrong:* "WHAT does everyone say?!" (curiosity = he pours another jar),
     or angry denial (denial is delicious; it repeats itself "louder").
2. **Taunt (bigger, bolder):** "Everyone NOW says the cat's the smart one and
   you're its ASSISTANT. The cat agreed. The cat AGREED, ask it."
   (the cat does not look up — this visibly destabilises Wisp)
   - *type.* Accept: the one-question ("Who told you that… who told THEM?"),
     bored rewrite ("Cat. Assistant. Groundbreaking. Lunch soon?").
     Reject: defending her intelligence, pleading with the cat on record.
3. **Taunt (going for the throat, misses, tries again):** "Everyone says —
   everyone says — …everyone says you're… QUIET. Aggressively quiet. It's WEIRD.
   THAT'S the rumour! …why isn't it landing?"
   - *type.* Accept: flipping the weak rumour ("Tell them I said hi."), dry
     reporter ("Source: everyone. Suspect source."). Reject: actually stung by
     "quiet" (feedback is kind about this: real words sting, that's not a fail —
     the jar just doesn't fill, retry).
- **Scene win:** every jar lids at once. Wisp, lidless and panicking, drops THE
  crate — and the jars all bear the same seal: a crown biting its own tongue.

**Scene 2 — The Author.** (pick → the reveal; then type, type)
4. **Taunt (terror-ramble, the twist mid-beat):** "NO NO — I don't WRITE them,
   I just DELIVER them! SHE writes them! Tall! Cold! HATES laughing, HATES quiet,
   hates your BOOT— the proclamations are all hers, look, LOOK—"
   - *best (pick):* "Who's she?" (the ONLY useful question in the book)
   - *ok:* "Then you're not gossip. You're post. Slow down, post."
   - *wrong:* chewing Wisp for delivering (he IS a scared courier; feedback:
     kicking the mail doesn't stop the letter).
   - **Mid-scene reveal:** nailed to every tree, the same proclamation in
     gothic script: **"WREN. DO NOT BE UNBOTHERED. — H.M."** The wood has a
     Queen. The rumors have an author. Echo: *"I know that seal. Everyone in
     this wood knows that seal. Nobody says it out loud. …I'll say it out loud."*
5. **Taunt (Wisp, braver now, doing his job badly on purpose):** "The Queen says
   everyone will be told you're the girl who TALKS TO HERSELF. What do I write?"
   - *type.* Accept: refusing to authorise ("You write 'no comment'."), or
     writer's-block judo ("Make it rhyme first. Can't release unrhymed gossip.").
     Reject: bargaining, rage at the Queen (early — she's still myth).
6. **Taunt (he panics and reads the real proclamation, tiny voice):**
   "…it says here 'DO NOT BE UNBOTHERED.' It's addressed to you. It's got a
   SPARKLE on it. …what do I do, what do I DO?"
   - *type.* Accept: calm command energy — "You? Deliver one change: 'no'." /
     to Echo: "Two spies are better than one." Reject: pretending the letter
     doesn't exist, meltdown fuel.
- **Scene win:** Wisp defects as a double courier (terrified, loyal to whoever's
  nearest, useful anyway). The chapter ends with the proclamation burning — and
  from somewhere very far away, a pair of opera glasses lowering.
- **Bridge (Sensei):** *"Most of what people say about people was written by
  someone with a plan. Before you react to the letter — check who wrote it."*
- **Reward:** 🫙 *Wisp, Decaf Gossip* · +40 🪙 · trophy *Follow the Money* ·
  the wood map opens: **the Court is visible now.**

### Chapter 5 · The Lake That Draws
**Teaches:** The Rewrite — aimed, for one chapter, at herself. **Villain:**
**The Sketch** — a pencil-drawing copy of Wren that crawls out of Mirror Lake,
mean, exaggerated, obviously false. **Meter:** graphite layers — a true-and-kind
line fades one layer.
**Arc note:** the gentlest chapter. The Sketch must always read as cartoon
caricature — its claims are absurd (never plausible, never the shape of real
cruelty heard at school). The inner-critic twist is stated in story and left
there.

**Setup:** Mirror Lake draws pictures of whatever looks in. This one finished
drawing and wandered off the surface. It has her hair. It does not have her manners.

**Scene 1 — The Copy.** (pick → type, type, type)
1. **Taunt (her own voice, wrong):** "Oh look, it's HER. The main character. She
   tries SO hard it's a SPORT."
   - *best (pick):* "Nice linework. A bit smudgy, but nice." (treat it as a
     drawing — Rewrite by deflating the medium, not the message)
   - *ok:* "You're a pencil drawing with opinions. Bold."
   - *wrong:* insulting it ("You're just SMUDGE!" — it's HER; every hit she throws
     draws blood on herself; meter rises), or arguing the claim ("I do NOT try
     hard—").
2. **Taunt (louder, doing her walk):** "Everyone's NICE to you out of PITY. I've
   seen the paperwork. There's a FORM."
   - *type.* Accept: rewrite the frame ("Pity gets you CAKES, apparently.
     Exploit it."), or flat "Form's filed under 'nonsense'. Check the index."
     Reject: believing-it energy (defence, hurt, interrogation of the Sketch —
     it feeds on exactly that).
3. **Taunt (the cruelest — which is absurd):** "You're the EXTRA in your own LIFE.
   There's a director. It's a SEAGULL. And the seagull's DISAPPOINTED."
   - *type.* Accept: comedy rewrite ("Tell the seagull I'm rewriting the script."),
     fog ("Probably. Seagulls would. Anyway—"). Reject: any engagement with
     "am I really like that" — feedback explicitly: *we don't eat from that jar.*
- **Scene win:** three layers faded; it's a rough sketch now, edges blurring
  into the lake's reflection.

**Scene 2 — The Mean Thing Inside.** (type ×5 — the chapter's heart; lifeline after
1 miss; Sensei unusually warm but still dry)
Sensei, before the scene: *"Parrot won't work — it's already you. Insults can't
work — they hit the wrong target. This one only starves on one thing: something
true, and kind, said to its face. Yes, its. It counts."*
4. **Taunt:** "You talk in your HEAD and the head is ME and I'm ALWAYS right."
   - *accept:* "You're my head. You're not the boss of it." / compliment the
     enemy to starve it: "You're tryin'." → one more layer gone.
5. **Taunt (smaller now, quieter):** "…you'll never be the coolest. I've seen the
   COOLEST. It's a deer. An actual deer."
   - *accept:* "Fair. Deer are objectively elite. I'm still pretty great though."
     (the game explicitly rewards kind-to-self — `best` detection accepts true
     + kind patterns)
6. **Taunt (last dregs, desperate):** "SAY SOMETHING NICE ABOUT YOU OR I'M DRAWING
   ANOTHER ONE OF YOU. MEANER."
   - *accept:* anything true and kind, however small ("I make good toast." is a
     FULLY valid `best` — the doc is serious: small and true beats grand and
     hollow).
   - *if 3 tries stall:* Sensei lends: "Try: 'I'm glad it's me in here.'"
- **Scene win:** the Sketch, fully faded, peels off the lake as a plain pencil
  drawing of DUCKS, wanders to the reeds, and stays. It's just art now.
- **Twist:** the lake draws the MEANEST version, because that's what looks in —
  Echo, quietly: "…she kept looking at it like it was the real one." Sensei does
  not develop this. He doesn't need to.
- **Bridge (Sensei):** *"The meanest echo in the wood is the one that speaks in
  your own voice. Same rule as always: don't eat what it hands you. And once in a
  while — feed it a kind true instead. It starves beautifully."*
- **Reward:** 🖍️ *Your Less Flattering Side* · +40 🪙 · trophy *Kind & True* ·
  Mirror Lake becomes a rest spot (rematch arena: the lake draws rematches).

### Chapter 6 · The Snigger Sisters
**Teaches:** The Dismissal under double pressure — one line answers two. **Villain:**
**Twin & Sprite**, the Queen's former Favourite Entertainment — identical girls,
identical lace-black parasols, one voice split mid-sentence. **Meter:** their
**audience seats** (tiny theatre seats that pop up as they perform) — every clean
line collapses a row.
**Arc note:** the two-girl mirror of the real situation, entirely in storybook
key: they are *performing* meanness as a double act, and the act dies without an
audience.

**Setup:** a path of perfect topiary, too-neat, every bush a different shade of
obedient. Two parasols rise like beetles. They finish each other's sentences and
each other's insults. They are *so* bored. Until Wren.

**Scene 1 — The Act.** (type ×3; one miss → lifeline)
1. **Taunt (in split stereo):** "Did you SEE —" "— her BOOT —" "one's green and
   one's— " "BOTH are green. Both. She's a WALK."
   - *accept:* treating them as one blob ("You two. One act. Not buying."), or
     flat verdict on the act ("Tight material. Work on the middle.").
     Reject: answering one sister (she takes it as a SIDE), defending the boots.
2. **Taunt (they rotate — one blocks, one flanks):** "Look at me when I'm being
   MEAN —" "— she's LOOKING at the TREE —" "TREE has better— " "the tree
   doesn't have her HAIR. WE do."
   - *accept:* watching both as one ("You're like one person on lag."), silence
     + keep walking (they MUST walk beside to keep the line running — out-walking
     them is literally the answer).
     Reject: picking the louder one to engage.
3. **Taunt (the trick — one suddenly NICE):** "Okay YOUR turn — say we're
   scary, then I'll say you're— " (they look at each other, script collapsing)
   "…that's not the bit. SAY the bit."
   - *accept:* refusing the script ("Casting's closed."), or flip ("You two keep
     forgetting whose play this is — nobody's.").
     Reject: playing along to keep peace (they both feed: it's ONE act).
- **Scene win:** the last row of seats collapses. They finish each other's
  silences — first time they've run out.

**Scene 2 — Demoted.** (type ×3; the twist)
4. **Taunt (wounded professionalism, the reveal mid-rant):** "Do you KNOW we were
   FAVOURITES? The Queen picked US from a hundred —" "— until Tuesday —"
   "— until the new twins got the BETTER lace —" "— and we're out here doing
   BOOT material for NO PAY— "
   - *accept:* to BOTH as one, bored-pity ("That's a management problem,
     love."), or rewrite the act ("Two favorites, demoted by TUESDAY.
     That's the real joke. Print it.").
     Reject: taking one side of their quarrel; pitying only one.
5. **Taunt (they try the Queen's defence, unprompted, scared even as they say it):**
   "You should be NICE to us —" "— she hears EVERYTHING —" "— she can't stand
   being— " "BEING WHAT—?" (they clamp both mouths shut at once. The weakness
   walks into the room uninvited: **the Queen cannot survive being ignored.**)
   - *accept:* filing it away with Echo ("Write that down. Say it quieter."),
     or dry ("Can't stand what, though?") — the question asked ONCE, flatly,
     not chased.
     Reject: storming off to "use" it, or demanding they repeat it (they won't —
     they're terrified, and the game respects that).
6. **Taunt (their last act, desperate, out of sync for the first time):**
   "FINE — we'll say you're ALONE —" "— we're NOT even real— " "we're hired— "
   "we're PASTRY-PAID— "
   - *accept:* the dismissal to end all dismissals — "Two acts, one audience,
     zero tickets. Love you both. BYE." (walk; the meter empties as they perform
     to an empty path).
- **Scene win:** the act dies mid-sentence, genuinely, in real time. The sisters
  stare at each other without a script — and Echo produces (from a holey pocket)
  two biscuits. *"The Circle meets Thursdays. Bring the parasols. They're
  technically umbrellas now."* Twin & Sprite defect for pastries and the radical
  novelty of being spoken TO.
- **Bridge (Sensei):** *"A show needs an audience. You are allowed to leave the
  theatre mid-show with your coat still on. It's the loudest thing in the room."*
- **Reward:** ☂️ *Twin & Sprite, Umbrellas Down* · +40 🪙 · trophy *Leave the
  Theatre* · **Echo & Wisp now have the Queen's weakness** — briefed on-screen:
  *"She cannot survive being ignored."* …*"Then we'd better get practising."*

### Chapter 7 · The Court of Mirrors
**Teaches:** Flattery as manipulation — deflect, owe nothing, walk. **Villain:**
**The Court of Mirrors** — a hall of tall mirrors that bow and compliment, each
mirror attached to a price. **Meter:** how low they bow — every deflected
compliment makes a mirror stand up straight (and crack, politely).
**Arc note:** the mirror-lake's courtly cousins; this is the Queen's antechamber —
everyone in it is auditioning.

**Setup:** the road ends at a hall of doors, all of them mirrors. The moment Wren
is seen, twelve bows happen at once. The pet immediately hates everyone here.
(Sensei: *"When the flattery arrives before you do, you're not a guest. You're a
resource."*)

**Scene 1 — Compliments with Strings.** (type ×3)
1. **Taunt (unison, reverent):** "YOUR MAJESTY — the wood's true Queen — surely
  you'll just tell us the NAME of one person you don't like —"
   - *accept:* verdict-flattery ("Flattery: noted. Access: denied."), the
     dry-parrot ("'Your Majesty.' Is that a title or a price?").
     Reject: naming anyone (they'd have her by lunchtime), or enjoying it
     visibly (a bow goes LOWER, meter fills).
2. **Taunt (one mirror, intimate, quieter than the rest):** "Your coat is the
   finest in the wood. May I just — TOUCH it? And perhaps borrow it. For a
   fortnight. And your name. I'd like to borrow your name."
   - *accept:* the absurd-refusal ("You may not borrow my name. It's got a
     payment plan."), fog ("It's dry-clean only. Everything's a no.")
     Reject: negotiating terms (any terms = the mirror gets a contract).
3. **Taunt (the group gasps, offended on her behalf, at each other):** "She
   doesn't even OWN a mirror! Which of you MISSED her?! SPEAK —"
   - *accept:* ending the bit ("Nobody missed me. I'm right here. Underdressed
     for yourselves, though."), walk-and-dismiss.
     Reject: defending the mirrors (they'll use her defence as a leash).
- **Scene win:** every mirror stands bolt upright at once. Upright mirrors are
  just… windows. Cracks form like polite applause.

**Scene 2 — The Crisis That Isn't.** (type ×3; the pet gag)
4. **Taunt (all mirrors, dramatic, the manufactured crisis):** "THE CAT. WE HAVE
   THE CAT. Come alone. Bring three compliments and a curtsy."
   (This is the chapter's test: panic is the price. Beat 1 of her reply is
   graded on calm only. Any panic-shaped reply → the mirrors purr, retry.)
   - *accept:* calm command ("You have the cat. The cat tolerates you. Keep him
     company while I finish walking."), or the verdict ("Kidnapping? In this
     economy? You'll return him before teatime.")
   - *wrong (always):* terror, bargaining, curtsying "just this once."
5. **The rescue (no taunt — a walk):** she finds the pet exactly as predicted:
   unharmed, asleep, a tiny bow tied round his collar, an empty saucer of cream
   with a mirror-apology note beneath: *"He said he'd only stay for the cream.
   He lied. We were honoured."* The bow stays. (It's a good bow.)
6. **Taunt (the last mirror, alone in the hall — it doesn't bow. It just says,
   simply):** "…your boots are very nice. That's all. No price. Good luck."
   - *accept:* **graceful thanks** — "Thank you. They are." (the KEY to the
     chapter: accepting a REAL compliment without ego AND without suspicion —
     "A true compliment is allowed to be true. Take it. Pay nothing.")
     Reject: suspicion ("What do you want?") — gently graded as almost-right:
     "The reflex is correct. This one was empty of traps. Try the thanks."
- **Scene win:** the hall doors unlock from the inside — the throne room. On
  every mirror, at the same time, the same reflection: not Wren. A tall woman,
  already seated, already watching, opera glasses down.
- **Bridge (Sensei):** *"Flattery is manipulation wearing its good coat. But the
  last one wasn't lying — and you took it like weather. Both skills. One day each."*
- **Reward:** 🪞 *The Court of Mirrors* · +40 🪙 · trophy *No Debts* ·
  the pet keeps the tiny bow (visible in every following scene and in the
  dojo pet sprite for Chapter 8 — yes, that's load-bearing cuteness).

### Chapter 8 · Queen Malvina the Magnificent
**Teaches:** Everything, at full power — and the one move no taunt can make:
**walking away.** **Villain:** **Queen Malvina** — tall, gothic, unhurried,
never shouts; her throne is upholstered with folded invitations (a lifetime of
"moments when someone shrank"). **Meter:** the height of her throne — every clean
line lowers it a step; the finale is decided by what she says, and then does.
**Difficulty:** all typed, 3 phases, strict `best`-or-lend finale. Completion is
guaranteed — the game would rather retire than trap her.

**Phase 1 — The Court Remembers** (type ×3): the wood's whole roster returns as
backing — an echo-wisp loops a sock joke, a wind-up giggle track, gossip banners,
two parasols at the back *watching, not helping* (they root for Wren now; the
Sketch watches from the lake in the distance). Each throws ONE recycled taunt
from Chapters 2–6. One line answers each: Fog, Rewrite, Dismissal — Sensei
coaches in the style of a corner man in the ninth round ("Loop was the socks
joke. You've done the socks joke. You've DONE the socks.").

**Phase 2 — The Kneel** (type ×2)
1. **"Everyone kneels eventually. Even the trees. Look —" (the trees kneel.
   All of them. It's ridiculous. One tree doesn't. He's embarrassed for
   everyone.)**
   - *accept:* irrelevance-judo ("Cool trees. Are we done with the weather
     bit?"), or the single green tree gets the chapter's only aside:
     "That one's got my number."
2. **"You will laugh at nothing, be cruel at no one, and LEAVE me. I command the
   last one most."** (she's honest, at last — she doesn't want a follower,
   she wants an audience large enough to feel big in)
   - *accept:* naming it, flat ("You don't want a subject. You want a seat
     in a theatre with your name on it. That's just loneliness in a hat.")

**Phase 3 — The Line** (type ×1–3): Sensei steps back. *"Don't insult her.
Don't argue with her. Don't perform for her. Say the thing that makes her
irrelevant — then walk. The walk is part of the line."*
- Any `best` line ends it. Approved shapes (examples shown as *options she can
  read but never forced to use*): "Have fun in here. We're done." / "You were
  the scariest thing in this wood until about four chapters ago." /
  "Thanks for the workout. Bye, Malvina." / anything dry, small, final.
- After 3 stalls, Sensei lends freely (unlimited, no stars penalty at this
  height — the book ends, full stop).
- **The walk:** the screen itself becomes the walk — the last screen IS clicking
  "keep walking" three times while Malvina monologues to her departing back:
  "— I haven't FINISHED — NO ONE has ever — I'll be in my THRONE of FOLDED
  INVITATIONS — do you HEAR me — ?" Silence. "…anyone?"

**Deflation ceremony:** she shrinks — not violently, deflatingly, like a grand
balloon let out slowly — until under the great crown there is simply a small
spare crown, sitting on the seat, slightly disappointed. Echo, reverently,
places the spare crown on the dojo pet. The pet allows it. (It is the only thing
the pet has ever allowed.)

**Epilogue — The Former Weirdos Support Circle:** picnic in the Gloomwood,
everyone former sits together — Toastbrig brings soup opinions, the Duchess is
still sat (waving cameo), Wisp delivers, Twin & Sprite perform a GOOD act for
one paying audience member (the Sketch, clapping politely, fully faded to a duck
drawing), the Court of Mirrors holds up snacks. The wood, checked once, is just
a wood now. Almost bedtime. It always was.

**The final Sensei lines (the whole book's landing — do not cut):**
*"That's the wood, then. Every monster in it ran on the same one thing: your
reaction. You learned to spend it like money — on purpose, or not at all.*
*And the truth the whole wood was built around, said plainly, at the end:*
***comebacks end scenes. Nobody out-wits a whole Queen — that's what grown-ups
are for.*** *You tell us, and we end it. That's not weakness. That's the chain
of command, and you're in it too."*
*(pause)* *"Now. About those socks."*

- **Rewards:** 👑 *Malvina, Slightly Deflated* · +80 🪙 · 🏆 trophy
  **Gloomwood Legend** · title **"The Unbothered"** unlocked FREE for the dojo
  · pet crown-glow (visible in the dojo, forever) · all chapters replay as
  rematches.
