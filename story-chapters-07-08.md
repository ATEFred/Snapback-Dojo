## Finale continuity: rules established before use

**IMPLEMENTATION ONLY — DO NOT NARRATE:** These are final story facts, revealed in Chapter 7's clock-service booklet:

1. The Great Clock has an emergency **loose second**. Stopping the outside face leaves one living moment uncaught. People/animals touching at that instant share it. It could have been anyone; Wren was touching Noodles as time stopped. This is a safety catch, not a reward for being special or unafraid. Touching frozen people afterwards cannot spread the exception.
2. That loose second opens a return/service door at its travellers' starting place. The clock's old safety mechanism made the door; the Queen did not invite Wren or arrange the adventure.
3. The Gloomwood lends shared speech to animals while they are there. The speech stays in that world. Noodles remains himself, with memories and affection, when he returns; he loses human speech, not his mind or his bond with Wren.
4. The Minute Key fits the Great Clock. Turning it to **RETURN** engages a one-way emergency release. The mechanism takes the key inside, permanently removes outside-world stopping control, and holds the first outside tick until the travellers cross home. This prevents Malvina stopping the world again and lets Wren get home to the exact interrupted breakfast without a rushed return journey.
5. The home door closes after Wren and Noodles are safely across. Its closure completes the release; the spoon falls, breakfast resumes. They are not stranded in either world. Normal story rewards and rematches remain available in the app without claiming the real-world cat can still speak.

**IMPLEMENTATION ONLY — DO NOT NARRATE:** The booklet's diagrams and spoken discussion establish these rules before the climax. The door's source and the speaking-cat mystery receive answers; they are not sequel bait. The Queen's power is real but limited: the safety catch exempts Wren/Noodles, and her local theatrical magic cannot compel consent. The ending is not a claim that a child can solve persistent real-world bullying by humiliating someone powerful.
